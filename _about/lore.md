So you've decided to read on. That's great, we'll be besties in no time.
My journey on the path to software engineering began when I enrolled in college. I didn't study computer science in undergrad, but I wanted to make mobile apps, specifically for the iOS platform. This was my first serious step into software development because I remember watching iOS courses from Stanford and entering the world of design patterns, architechtures, systems design and more. Throughout all of undergrad I built projects of various scales but didn't go beyond the mobile world.

Enter the coronavirus pandemic. I remember taking the train back to Brooklyn from Manhattan one of
those days and being messages by a close friend about a master's program at New York University for computer science that allowed students with no undergraduate experience in the subject to apply. I remember applying that same night for shits and giggles. Next thing we knew, we both got accepted to the Tandon Bridge program and then were accepted to the full master's course. The rest as they say is history.

Since then, I've touched about every relevant language I could have. From Python to Java, to Ruby on Rails, even some Angular. I prefer jumping around languages and staying afloat with the latest and greatest, but I always settle on a language I need for a particular problem. I guess I am a Jack of all trades and a master of none, and I like it that way.
