I have long wanted to try my hand at making a game. However, after flirting with the idea in highschool, I also knew how daunting of a task it is, and why entire billion dollar teams are tasked with making the latest triple-A hits. There's the scriptwriting, the lighting, the character modeling, the animation, the music...the list goes on and on. Nevertheless, computing power continues to increase across the board and even mobile devices now house the latest in fancy graphical technology; from specialized GPU chips to ray-tracing technology. And with doomsday headlines preaching of AI replacing all of mankind's work, I decided to embrace my artistic side and pretend I am a well-off Roman citizen in the days of old, practicing nothing but art and drinking. Thus, I have found myself squarely in the world of Blender (primarily because I'm a cheap bastard). All earlier attempts to use blender have ended with me learning nothing more than how to make loop cuts before deciding there are more enjoyable things to waste my time on. Oh how wrong I was. Finally getting that a-ha moment when I learn the shortcuts and workflow of a blender project fills me with the same kind of rush and enjoyment I got back in the days from learning to code. Not that seasoned coding veteran feeling of learning new syntax but that "mom, look what I made!" moment when a 3D object actual looks in a rendered scene how you want it to.
